Previsão semanal de casos de dengue em Curitiba com redes LSTM: casos, clima e intervalos de previsão

Original article in Portuguese.

Fernando Aramis Colque[ Especialização em IA Generativa Aplicada · UTFPR ]
Course: IAGA04 – Deep Learning • Submitted: 21 Oct 2026 • Published online: Nov 2026

Abstract. The 2024 dengue epidemic took Curitiba from 913 probable cases in 2023 to over 15,000 in the first half of 2024. This work evaluates Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks to forecast weekly probable cases one to four weeks ahead. Three network versions were compared — cases only (A), cases and climate with seasonality (B) and cases with seasonality (C) — plus naïve forecasts, over the 2022/23 and 2023/24 test seasons. In 2022/23 the univariate LSTM cut the mean absolute error by 14% at four weeks and had well-calibrated 90% intervals (94–98% coverage). Climate variables worsened performance. In the 2023/24 epidemic, whose peak was nearly three times the training maximum, all networks underestimated the magnitude. The LSTM is useful short-term within the historical range but does not replace epidemiological judgement in extreme events.

Keywords:denguetime-series forecastingLSTMconformal predictionepidemiological surveillance
Motivation

1 Introduction

Dengue has a strong seasonal component, linked to temperature and rainfall, which favour Aedes aegypti reproduction and viral replication. In 2024 Brazil recorded about 6.6 million probable cases and 6,199 deaths [Chen and Moraga, 2025], with the disease spreading south [Araujo et al., 2026]. Curitiba, subtropical and historically low-transmission, went from 913 probable cases in 2023 to 15,389 in the first half of 2024 alone, nearly double 2016 (7,883), the previous peak.

Anticipating the curve by a few weeks helps plan beds, field teams and control campaigns. The goal is to check whether an LSTM [Hochreiter and Schmidhuber, 1997] beats naïve forecasts for weekly probable cases in Curitiba, one to four weeks ahead, and whether climate variables improve the forecast.

Method

2 Data and methods

We used the official 2024 Sprint dataset [Coelho et al., 2024]: probable SINAN cases organized by InfoDengue, and weekly ERA5 reanalysis climate. Curitiba (IBGE code 4106902) yielded 753 weeks (3 Jan 2010 to 2 Jun 2024); the last four, incomplete due to reporting delay, were excluded, leaving 749. Target and main input are log(1 + cases), Min-Max normalized on the training set; the week of year was encoded with sine and cosine.

12

Input window

Each forecast uses the previous 12 weeks of cases (and, optionally, climate and seasonality) to project t+1 to t+4.

A·B·C

Recurrent network

Two LSTM layers (64 and 32) with Dropout 0.2 and a dense output. Trained with Adam and loss over log(1+cases), early stopping.

E1·E2

Uncertainty

90% intervals via adaptive conformal prediction: the band widens when recent errors grow.

3×

Data

Probable SINAN cases via InfoDengue and ERA5 climate via Mosqlimate (Sprint 2024). Curitiba: IBGE code 4106902.

Reading: the LSTM beats the naïve within the historical range (E1); in the out-of-range epidemic (E2), copying the last week errs less — the expected limit of models trained on already-observed levels.

Gain within the historical range

In the 2022/23 season, the univariate LSTM tracked the rise and fall of the curve and cut the error by up to 20% at four weeks (14% averaged over 3 seeds).

Climate hurt

With ~600 training windows, the climate variables added more noise than signal (overfitting). Seasonality alone kept the performance.

The 2024 epidemic escaped

The peak of 1,480 cases fell outside the training range (1.16 on the normalized scale). All networks underestimated, and the naïve forecast had the lowest error.

The intervals warned

Even missing the magnitude, the conformal intervals widened with the epidemic and came to contain much of the peak — signaling the uncertainty.

Where it goes

4 Limitations and next steps

01

Regional model

Use neighboring and similar municipalities as predictors (pooled model) to extend each city's short series.

02

Forecast growth

Model the growth rate instead of the absolute level, to avoid saturating in outbreaks outside the historical range.

03

Lagged climate

Select temperature (~5-week lag, r=0.46) and rainfall (~8-week lag, r=0.26) instead of the raw variables that caused overfitting.

04

Ensembles

Combine models, as the Ministry of Health's surveillance began doing from Sprint 2024 on.

Conclusion

5 Conclusion

The univariate LSTM is useful short-term within the historical range: it cut the error by 14% at four weeks and produced well-calibrated 90% intervals. Raw climate variables worsened the forecast. Epidemics outside the range, like 2024, are not anticipated by the point forecast — but the widened uncertainty band signals the risk, and epidemiological judgement remains indispensable.
